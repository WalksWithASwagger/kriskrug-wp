How I got here

I’ve spent a lot of my life moving between people who don’t usually find themselves in the same room. Here are some of the places that shaped the work.

From 1995 The open web

I started building websites and communities in 1995. I worked on the Grateful Dead’s site and on open-source publishing with Bryght and Drupal. I fell in love with a web that people could make together, and I’ve been chasing that feeling ever since.

Explore the work ↗
Documentary years Learning to pay attention

In 2010 I went to the Gulf of Mexico with the TEDxOilSpill expedition. I photographed the Vancouver Olympics that year, and the next year I joined the Midway Journey. Each assignment meant walking into a place with a story already in motion.

Photography taught me to listen when I get there. A camera can get you into a room. You still have to earn the picture.

Galiano Island · 2021 The school garden

On Galiano Island I coordinated the school garden for the Food Program. I brought children from the Activity Centre summer program into the garden. We ended the summer with fries and ketchup made from the potatoes and tomatoes we’d grown there.

Vancouver, now Building rooms for AI

I started Vancouver AI because I wanted a room where artists and engineers could show each other what they were making and argue about what it meant. The meetups grew into BC + AI and helped make Futureproof possible.

Today I co-teach Responsible AI Professional with Martin Lopatka and Sarah Downey. I’ve written about the Vancouver AI community with Patrick Pennefather and David Gaertner, and helped bring community recommendations to a federal consultation.

Through our fellowship with Internet Archive Canada, we’re supporting builders working with Canada’s public record.
