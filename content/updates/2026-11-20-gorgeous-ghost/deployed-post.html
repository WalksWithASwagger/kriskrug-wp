I kept a shot of a lightning bolt hitting a field of dead television sets. I threw out plenty of other shots that looked expensive as hell. The lightning stayed because it tells you what kind of world you're in before anybody has to explain it. The screens are dead. Something out there is still trying to get through.

That's Gorgeous Ghost: 69.42 seconds of glam space opera made for the BC + AI Film Club Prompt Challenge. A transmission from Andromeda drops a prophet into a switched-off world. He needs a band to wake the place up. He gets one. For a minute there, it looks like a very bad idea to let any of them near the volume knob.

From a melting whale to a band

The first short I made for the club was I AM NOMAD, with Suzy Easton. That one had an airport full of flying fish and a lesson I got to learn the stupid way: I rebuilt the footage and nearly lost the film because I moved things off the song. The shots looked better on their own. Played together, they had no pulse.

I locked the song first this time. That gave me something to say no with. If the shot missed the beat, I could find a better few seconds inside it, or I could lose it. A better-looking shot could still be the wrong shot. I rated the generated footage, then scrubbed through the keepers for the exact frames where a look or a gesture could carry the cut.

I was Ziggy, the doomed prophet with the lightning bolt across his face. Magenta Rune was the Receiver, the face I want you to watch when the world comes back on. Mayumi Rawlings was the Space Boss, who looks like she actually knows what all those switches do. Kevin Friel was Static, with a guitar and no apparent interest in helping anybody get a clean signal. Noise gets to be in the band. That felt right.

That's my favourite thing about having a Film Club. You can bring the work in while it's still weird and figure out what it is around other people who make things. There are photos from the July meetup if you want to see what that room actually looks like.

What happened at twenty-one seconds

Watch the first few seconds with the sound on. There's a lot of blue and a lot of room around the figures. I held those early shots longer, and I kept the palette narrow even when I had louder material sitting right there. The blast of colour at the chorus would have meant nothing if the whole first verse had already been a fireworks show.

At about 0:21, the rhythm changes and the frame opens up. Ziggy throws his arms out in front of that ridiculous many-pointed sun. A couple of beats later it's Magenta's eyes doing the work. Then a gold car shoots sideways through the picture. I wanted your eye to have somewhere new to go as the song got bigger.

This is where the movie gets tender for me. All that chrome and lightning, and what I really wanted was the feeling of somebody waking up enough to be seen. I didn't want a speech about it. I wanted it to come through in the cut to a face.

Get out while it's loud

The brightest beat is over in under three seconds. There's a whole other shitty film where I keep the pyrotechnics going because I have the shots and now you have to look at them. In this one the heat drops out. Ziggy ends up slumped in a chair, still in costume, with nobody cheering. Then he turns away and the picture starts to let him go.

AI generated the footage. I chose the takes and where to enter and leave them, built the cold-to-warm grade, and finished the cut with grain, flashes and sound. I can show you exactly where those decisions are. That feels more useful than the argument over what to put on a business card, although we've had that argument at Film Club too.

A place to go after the credits

On gorgeousghost.com the film has a busted-looking broadcast console of its own. The screen changes with the cut. There's a four-channel tuner, a transcript and Signal Atlas, and a switch to dial down the effects. The Atlas puts 29 moments on the clock. Go back to the colour change and see if you agree with where I put it. That's what I wanted the site to be good for: you can make your own call about the edit without taking my word for it.
