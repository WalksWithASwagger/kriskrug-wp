# Prepare your AI work brief

Pick one piece of work you actually need to do. Give it ten minutes and leave with a brief you can use with your team or your chosen AI tool.

At North House, Kris showed a habit: give each project a home and gather the context it needs. This exercise starts with the brief. You do not need a GitHub account to try it.

[Read the North House recap](https://kriskrug.co/2026/09/03/what-i-showed-founders-about-ai-workflows/).

## Before you start

Choose a small task with an outcome you can check. Describe your sources without pasting private material. Leave out passwords, personal information and anything you do not have permission to share.

You can write “not decided” or “not available.” A gap you can see is something you can act on.

## 1. What are you trying to accomplish?

Name the thing you want to make or the decision you need to prepare. Keep it small enough to review in one sitting.

## 2. Who is this for?

Name the reader or user. What do they already know, and what should they be able to do with the result?

## 3. What sources or context are available?

List the documents, examples or facts the work needs. Say what you have permission to use, what is missing and where an answer still needs checking. Listing a source does not give this exercise or another tool access to it.

## 4. What would a useful result satisfy?

Write a few conditions someone could check. Include the format, the essential information and how you will know it is ready. “Make it better” leaves a lot to guess.

## 5. What must remain private or outside the task?

Name what must not be shared, assumed or done. Include any actions that need separate permission. If you have not checked a source's sharing permission, keep its contents out.

## 6. What decision or check must remain with a person?

Name the person or role who will review the result, what they must check and what they must approve before anyone acts on it.

## Teaching example: a neighbourhood photo walk

**Newly authored, fictional example.** This is a teaching aid inspired by the recap's habit of gathering context. It is not a recovered demonstration, participant brief, testimonial or account of an actual event.

### Objective

Draft a one-page invitation for a free neighbourhood photo walk. Prepare copy for review; do not publish or send it.

### Audience

Local adults who enjoy taking pictures, including people using phones. They should be able to decide whether the walk suits them and know what to bring.

### Available sources

For this fictional exercise, assume I have an organizer-approved route outline and a public transport map. The meeting time and step-free route check are still missing. Mark them as unresolved; do not invent them.

### Success conditions

Keep the invitation under 250 words. Include the purpose, what to bring, the proposed route and the unresolved time and access checks. Use plain language. Do not describe the route as accessible until it has been checked.

### Privacy and exclusions

Do not include private phone numbers, participant names or unapproved photographs. Do not promise equipment, permissions or access arrangements that have not been confirmed. Do not contact anyone or publish the invitation.

### Human review

The walk organizer checks the route, timing, access information and image permissions. They approve the final invitation before it is shared.

## Read your brief once more

Could another person start from this? Check the sources and the gaps before you copy it into another tool. That tool's privacy rules will apply when you share it there.

Keep the parts you know. Mark the parts you do not. You can save an unfinished draft and come back with better information.

## Interface wording for the proof

- Start: **Prepare my brief**
- Preview: **Preview my brief**
- Copy: **Copy brief**
- Download: **Download Markdown**
- Print: **Print worksheet**
- Reset: **Clear my answers**
- Reset confirmation: **Clear all six answers? Save a copy first if you want to keep them.**
- Empty field in output: **Not provided. Resolve before relying on this brief.**
- Incomplete preview: **Your draft has unanswered questions. You can still save it. Missing answers stay marked.**
- Copy success: **Brief copied.**
- Copy failure: **Copy did not work. Select the text below and copy it, or download your brief.**
- No JavaScript: **Use the worksheet below. Write your answers in a document or print a copy.**
- Footer: **Context brief · Method version 0.1-draft · Inspired by the North House recap.** Link “North House recap” to the public article above.
