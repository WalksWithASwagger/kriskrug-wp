## BC + AI Ecosystem: the Public Compute angle

### LinkedIn + Facebook

Data centres are the part of AI you can't demo. They're substations, cooling, water, fibre, and a great deal of electricity, and British Columbia is deciding right now which proposals advance toward scarce clean power.

Our executive director Kris Krüg joined Bob Mackin on theBreaker.news podcast, with Dave Olson, to talk about what that means for the rest of the ecosystem. Recorded outside the H.R. MacMillan Space Centre after July's Vancouver AI meetup, so it sounds like two people on the grass rather than a panel.

We built the Public Compute Project for this exact moment. Five questions, asked of every proposal: who owns it, what does it consume, who pays for the load, what stays in British Columbia, and who holds the decision. It's a public-interest framework and a pilot registry. It isn't a government list, a regulator, or a decision authority, and we say so on the page.

Have a look. Tell us what we're missing.

https://bc-ai.ca/public-compute

Episode: https://thebreaker.news/opinion/podcast-460/

## Vancouver AI: the meetup angle

### LinkedIn + Facebook

Meetup #31 got written up.

Bob Mackin brought theBreaker.news podcast to the H.R. MacMillan Space Centre on Wednesday July 29, then sat outside on the grass with Kris Krüg and Dave Olson for 23 minutes.

Dave came in from Okayama, Japan, where a 150 year old storehouse barn is now his mixed media studio. He's the reason this meetup has a podcast at all, because he kept asking us to tell him what was happening over here. His ask of AI is refreshingly small: repair a 5,600 post archive, fix the broken links across every site we all built in 1995, then handle the mundane. He'd like to keep writing the poems himself.

That's the room. Artists, builders, teachers, policy people, and plenty who are still deciding what they think, in one place on a Wednesday night.
