I made a 69.42-second glam space opera because apparently a normal short film was not enough.

[Gorgeous Ghost](https://gorgeousghost.com/) is a film, a song, a public transcript and a [Signal Atlas](https://gorgeousghost.com/#atlas) you can poke around in after the lights go down. Planet Andromeda speaks in lightning. Civilization is wobbling. The chorus still absolutely slaps.

It's my second film for the [BC + AI Film Club](https://bc-ai.ca/communities/film-club) prompt challenge. The first was [I AM NOMAD](https://kriskrug.co/2026/07/18/i-am-nomad-ai-film/), a two-hander with [Suzy Easton](https://www.suzyeaston.ca/bio/). Same club, new prompt, same rule: take it personally, take it too far, and finish. This time the thing wanted a band.

## The ghost is a band

The credits card keeps it simple. Created by Kris Krüg. The Scallywags: **Kevin Friel, Magenta Rune and Mayumi Rawlings**. I made the film and assembled the signal, but it reads better as a band than as a solo demo. That's the useful part of making things in public. The strange little object picks up fingerprints from the people around it.

The film runs on four characters, four frequencies, one transmission:

- **Ziggy**, the doomed Starman prophet. That's my channel.
- **Magenta Rune**, the Receiver.
- **Mayumi**, the Space Boss.
- **Kevin**, the Static. In this band, noise is a musician too.

Every channel gets its own colour, its own signal art and its own place in the mix. On the site you can tune all four yourself and lock the band in one frequency at a time.

> Four people on one film is a crowd unless each one owns a channel. Then it's a band.

Special thanks in the transmission go to **Kaoru Yoshihira** and **[Floyo.ai](https://www.floyo.ai/)**.

## Song first

Rule one on this production: the song was locked before the edit began, and the song became the edit map. Cold open, door, bloom, peak, fall, wink. The picture never leads the music.

The broadcast arrives cold. Ziggy crosses a switched-off world while the other channels start to answer. I held the early shots long and kept the palette in a narrow band of blue, so every bit of colour after that has to be earned.

## The colour move

Here's the one craft call I'd tattoo on the inside of my own eyelids. The world stays cold blue until the chorus. Then it breaks open into full colour.

At 21 seconds the door gives way. Receiver rings, static, command pulses and gold light pile into a band, and the edit speeds up through matched gestures while the palette opens one frequency at a time.

Then the peak: one bright instant where the transmission hits maximum voltage and the whole world is visible. It runs under three seconds. It's the shortest, loudest phrase in the film, and it gets out before the spectacle goes soft.

Then the fall. Heat drains out of the frame and intimacy replaces scale. A hard tonal drop after the peak gives the ending weight instead of one more crescendo. The last look breaks into glitter and slips back through the receiver. Far out.

## Hand-finished

The machine handled parts of the generative middle. It didn't decide what the film was, which shots survived, where the song turned, how long the silence should last, or whether the thing had any pulse. I picked the footage, cut it to the beat and finished it with texture, light and sound. Those calls stayed with the ear, the eye, the edit and the bullshit detector.

The Signal Atlas shows the work. Seven chapters, 29 moments, each one pinned to its timestamp and its line of the song, with a note on why it sits where it does. A patient opening hold makes the first interruption feel discovered instead of introduced. That's directing. It just happens after the generation instead of before it.

I still don't have a clean job title for that. [Nobody does yet](https://kriskrug.co/2026/07/28/no-one-knows-what-to-call-us-yet/).

> AI is useful when it gives me more surfaces to react against. It's less interesting when it asks to be mistaken for the artist.

## A song with a body around it

The film was only one door. [gorgeousghost.com](https://gorgeousghost.com/) is built like a console on Andromeda, channel 04. A liquid signal field shimmers behind everything, and it runs on the film's own timecode. When the chorus blooms on screen, the page blooms with it. At the peak the field hits full voltage. At the fall the glitch drains away.

Inside the console:

- a four-channel tuner for locking in the band
- the Signal Atlas, every moment and why it landed there
- a Signal Lab full of playable instruments
- a Relic button that forges you a keepsake from the signal
- a full transcript, captions and a reduce-effects switch, so the whole thing stays reachable if motion isn't your friend

A door nobody opened should still open for everybody.

The song also broadcasts on [Ghost Radio](https://ghost.radio.fm/), my pirate little audio lab. Gorgeous Ghost has its own station at 100.7, three tracks deep, next to the other records, half-finished albums and strange frequencies. A film should be allowed to leak into the radio.

## Field notes

For anyone cutting their next short for the club:

- **Lock the song first.** In a piece carried by music, the track is the architecture. Name your phases before you cut a frame.
- **Withhold colour on purpose.** A narrow palette is a savings account. Spend it all on the chorus.
- **Give every collaborator a frequency.** Their own colour, their own art, their own spot in the mix.
- **Peak short, fall hard, end soft.** The brightest moment works because it leaves early. The ending lands because the heat drains first.
- **Build the world the film lives in.** A console, a tuner, an atlas. The site is the rest of the transmission.
