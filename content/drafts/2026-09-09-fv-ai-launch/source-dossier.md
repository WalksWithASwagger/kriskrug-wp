# FV+AI Source Dossier

## Executive read

FV+AI is not a cold start. It grows from approximately ten Surrey gatherings, Darren Coleman's local business and Toastmasters network, and the regional-chapter pattern already proven in Comox Valley. Darren has offered the first venue, food, drinks, and local operational leadership. Kris has already approved the official BC + AI banner, the FV+AI shorthand, and the long-form name.

The intended first room is deliberately small: roughly 20 local business owners, practitioners, and AI-curious neighbours. The format should be practical and conversational rather than a Coleman Technologies sales event.

## Source timeline

### June 12 — origin email

Darren described strong AI interest at Walnut Grove Toastmasters and a geographic gap: Vancouver is too far for many people in Langley and the wider Fraser Valley. He explicitly said he wanted to complement Vancouver AI and BC + AI rather than create a competing group.

Kris replied that the Surrey community had already created a foothold of roughly ten events and proposed collaborating with Darren and Matthew Schwartzman, with six to eight weeks of lead time.

**Decision signal:** regional expansion should build on Surrey, not erase it.

### June 19–22 — working call and venue proposal

The June 19 call was not recorded in the knowledge base. Darren's June 22 follow-up documents the outcome:

- Coleman Technologies office as the pilot venue
- 20178 96 Ave, Suite C400, Langley
- approximately 20 people, invite-only
- pizza, pop, and water in the boardroom
- main room for presentation and conversation
- initial thought of 7:00 PM
- interest in a recurring monthly series

An existing knowledge-base draft recommended doors/food at 6:30 PM, content at 7:00 PM, one short anchor, round-the-room introductions, open discussion, and wrap by 8:30 PM.

### July 8–15 — operating model and official approval

Darren proposed July 28, 6:00–8:00 PM, with this run of show:

- 6:00 — doors, pizza, drinks, mingling
- 6:30 — welcome and purpose
- 6:40 — local business owner use case
- 7:00 — practitioner demo
- 7:20 — open roundtable
- 7:50 — wrap and next date
- 8:00 — done

He offered Coleman Technologies as sponsor of the first few events: space, pizza, and drinks, with “no pitch” and a community-first tone.

Kris rejected July 28 because it collided with a Vancouver Meetup week and established two rules:

1. Regional chapter nights do not run during Vancouver Meetup week.
2. A new chapter needs six weeks from live invite to doors open.

Kris approved:

- official BC + AI status
- **FV+AI** as shorthand
- **BC + AI Fraser Valley Community Meetup** as long form
- BC + AI promotion once a viable date was set
- speaker help
- Darren connecting directly with Matthew

Kris also renamed the Surrey group to Surrey and Fraser Valley and made Darren an admin.

Darren proposed September 2 or September 9 and added Matthew to the thread. He described the audience as local business owners, people already using AI, and curious newcomers. He suggested Matthew speak at the first event about the Surrey community.

### July 31 — Office Hours commitment

Darren said: “I think you finally agreed on September 9th. You're going to help me get the Fraser Valley plus AI going.”

Kris committed to a Langley planning session before launch. Darren reported that his BC + AI profile/login was still broken and said there were already many interested people before any public promotion. The meeting action list captured three launch tasks:

- fix Darren's member profile/login loop
- set up the FV+AI Luma page for September 9
- hold an in-person Langley planning session

### August 5–10 — Luma and press release escalation

Darren asked whether Kris was creating the Luma page and requested BC + AI branding and rollout direction. He also offered to use an EIN Presswire credit expiring August 12.

On August 10, Darren sent a two-page press release and said he planned to submit it later that day. The release announces September 9 at the Coleman Technologies office and positions Darren as founder of FV+AI.

## Existing chapter lessons

### Surrey

- Approximately 30 attendees works for a mature regional room.
- Circle discussion and short demos are more valuable than a long lecture.
- Local business, students, builders, and practitioners create productive cross-pollination.
- Matthew and the Surrey group should be credited as the direct predecessor, not folded out of the story.

### Comox Valley

- The durable ownership split is local lead plus ecosystem connector.
- A regional room becomes real when it can run without Kris physically present.
- Same-day recaps, shared Luma infrastructure, and a local volunteer core help the chapter compound.
- A distinct local identity matters; a chapter should not feel like a Vancouver satellite.
